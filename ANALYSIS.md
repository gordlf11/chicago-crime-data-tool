# Checking Mayor Johnson's carjacking/robbery numbers

**Date:** 2026-11-19 (Chicago time)
**Source:** Post from `chicagosmayor` (Threads/X), posted ~2026-11-18:
> "We got record low carjackings before GTA 6."
> Graphics: **"CARJACKINGS DOWN 76% under Mayor Johnson"** (labeled "2022 YTD" → "2026 YTD") / **"ROBBERIES DOWN 57% under Mayor Johnson"** (labeled "2023 YTD" → "2026 YTD"), plus a separate **"Record low carjackings"** graphic.

Note the two percentage graphics use *different* starting years — carjackings compares to 2022, robberies to 2023 — both ending at "2026 YTD." That's easy to miss since both small-print labels just say "YTD" at a glance.

**Data source:** City of Chicago Data Portal, ["Crimes – 2001 to Present"](https://data.cityofchicago.org/Public-Safety/Crimes-2001-to-Present/ijzp-q8t2) (`ijzp-q8t2`), queried directly via the public SoQL API on 2026-11-19. This is the same dataset the mayor's office draws from. The dataset's most recent available date is **2026-11-11** (it excludes the most recent 7 days as a standing policy), so "2026 YTD" below means Jan 1–Nov 11, 2026, matched to the same Jan 1–Nov 11 window in the comparison year.

## Summary

| Claim | Stated | Matches city data? |
|---|---|---|
| Carjackings down 76% (2022 YTD → 2026 YTD) | −76% | **Yes** — actual is −77.8% |
| Robberies down 57% (2023 YTD → 2026 YTD) | −57% | **Yes** — actual is −56.6% |
| "Record low carjackings" | implied all-time | **No** — 2013–2015 were each lower, matched to the same point in the year |

## 1. Carjackings down 76%?

Chicago files carjacking under `primary_type = "ROBBERY"`, with `description` = `VEHICULAR HIJACKING` or `AGGRAVATED VEHICULAR HIJACKING` — it's not its own top-level crime category in the city's classification system.

| Period | Carjackings |
|---|---|
| 2022, Jan 1–Nov 11 | 1,422 |
| 2026, Jan 1–Nov 11 | 316 |
| **Change** | **−77.8%** |

The claimed −76% is close to exact — within 2 points, and the real number is slightly *better* than claimed, not worse.

## 2. Robberies down 57%?

| Period | Robberies (includes carjacking, per the city's own classification) |
|---|---|
| 2023, Jan 1–Nov 11 | 9,414 |
| 2026, Jan 1–Nov 11 | 4,090 |
| **Change** | **−56.6%** |

The claimed −57% matches almost exactly.

## 3. "Record low carjackings"?

This is the one claim that doesn't hold up. Comparing the same Jan 1–Nov 11 window across every year since 2001 (so 2026's partial year is measured fairly against the same point in other years, not against their full-year totals):

| Year | Carjackings (Jan 1–Nov 11) | | Year | Carjackings (Jan 1–Nov 11) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 490 | | 2019 | 501 |
| 2011 | 499 | | 2020 | 1,127 |
| 2012 | 421 | | 2021 | 1,520 |
| 2013 | 290 | | 2022 | 1,422 |
| **2014** | **256** | | 2023 | 1,115 |
| 2015 | 271 | | 2024 | 839 |
| 2016 | 512 | | 2025 | 430 |
| 2017 | 752 | | **2026** | **316** |
| 2018 | 640 | | | |

**2026's 316 is a real, large improvement — the lowest since 2015 — but not a record.** 2013 (290), 2014 (256), and 2015 (271) were all lower through this same point in the year. This holds whether you compare matched year-to-date windows (above) or full calendar years (2013: 344, 2014: 303 — still both below any recent year).

## 4. A separate question: what does "under Mayor Johnson" actually cover?

The graphics' baseline years (2022 for carjacking, 2023 for robbery) are each the locally worst recent year for that specific crime type — which is a defensible way to show "peak to now," but it's also worth knowing what it does and doesn't capture about Johnson's own tenure specifically, since neither baseline year change is entirely about decisions made after he took office.

Brandon Johnson was sworn in **May 15, 2023**. Splitting the data at his actual inauguration date, using full 12-month "mayoral years" so no comparison crosses a season unevenly:

| Period | Carjackings | Robberies |
|---|---|---|
| Year before he took office (5/15/22–5/14/23) | 1,477 | 9,426 |
| Johnson Year 1 (5/15/23–5/14/24) | 1,222 | 11,081 |
| Johnson Year 2 (5/15/24–5/14/25) | 743 | 7,910 |

Robbery was higher in Johnson's first mayoral year than in the year before he arrived, before falling sharply in year 2 — the decline is concentrated in the more recent part of his term rather than spread evenly across it. That doesn't contradict the 76%/57% figures above (those measure a different, longer window, back to each category's peak year), but it's useful context for what "under Mayor Johnson" is doing in the phrasing. ## 5. Independent cross-check: CPD's own published numbers

CPD publishes its own monthly/annual "in review" press releases directly (`chicagopolice.org/wp-content/uploads/...`) — a second, independent source generated by the city itself. They corroborate this analysis closely:

| Metric | This analysis (SoQL query) | CPD's own press release |
|---|---|---|
| Carjackings, full year 2024 | 951 | **957**, "down 26% compared to 2023" ([2024 in Review](https://www.chicagopolice.org/wp-content/uploads/2024-in-Review.pdf), released 1/3/2025) |
| Robbery, 2024 vs. 2023 | −17.5% | **"down 17% compared to 2023"** (same release) |
| Carjackings, Jan 1–Nov 30, 2025 vs. same period 2024 | −48.9% | 454 carjackings, **"down 48% compared to this time in 2024"** ([November 2025 in Review](https://www.chicagopolice.org/wp-content/uploads/November-2025-in-Review.pdf), released 12/3/2025) |
| Robbery, Jan 1–Nov 30, 2025 vs. 2024 | −35.6% | **"35% year to date decline"** (same release) |
| Robbery, full year 2025 vs. 2024 | −36.2% | **"36% year to date decline"** ([2025 in Review](https://www.chicagopolice.org/wp-content/uploads/2025-in-Review.pdf), released 1/5/2026) |

Every figure matches within about a percentage point — strong validation that directly querying the public dataset reproduces the city's own official statistics, and that the method used here (SoQL queries against the raw dataset) is sound.

Worth noting: CPD's own routine releases always compare to the immediately preceding year. The mayor's graphics instead measure from each category's own worst recent year, which is a different (and less standard) choice, even though — per Sections 1–2 — the resulting math checks out.

## Caveats

- **"2026 YTD" here means Jan 1–Nov 11, 2026** — the latest date in the dataset as of this check (it excludes the most recent 7 days as standing policy). The post's actual cutoff date is unknown and may differ slightly; a few more or fewer days of data could move the percentages by a point or so.
- **Data is preliminary and can be revised.** CPD's own documentation: "preliminary crime classifications may be changed at a later date based upon additional investigation."
- **The data owner's own disclaimer**: the dataset's official description states *"the information should not be used for comparison purposes over time."* Standard boilerplate CPD attaches to this dataset in general, not a statement that any specific analysis here is invalid — but worth citing alongside any numbers pulled from it.
- **IUCR classification changes over time** could affect historical comparability; this check did not audit for that.
- Murders are **not** included in this dataset's crime counts the same way other categories are — not relevant to carjacking/robbery, but worth knowing if extending this analysis.

## Methodology

**Two independent ways of pulling this:**
1. Queried `https://data.cityofchicago.org/resource/ijzp-q8t2.json` directly with SoQL (`$select`/`$where`/`$group`) for exact matched year-to-date windows, filtered by `primary_type` and `description`. Full reusable client: [chicago-crime-data-tool](https://github.com/gordlf11/chicago-crime-data-tool). No API key required — see that repo's README.
2. Cross-checked against CPD's own published "in review" press releases (PDFs, fetched and read directly) — Section 5.

Also reviewed the dataset's own "About this data" documentation on the portal (column definitions, update frequency, disclaimers) — folded into the Caveats above.
